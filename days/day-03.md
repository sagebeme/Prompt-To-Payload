# Day 3: Be specific, then iterate

**Level 1 · Foundations** · about 60 minutes · no coding

> You ask for "a short post about our new product". The AI writes 400 words, five hashtags and three rocket emojis. To the AI, that *was* short. You meant one sentence for LinkedIn that your boss won't cringe at.
>
> Then you type "no, shorter", and it cuts it to 300 words. You type "SHORTER", and it apologises and gives you 250. Twenty minutes gone.
>
> Two skills fix this: saying exactly what you mean, and giving feedback like an editor instead of a frustrated customer.

## Today's goal

Replace vague words with measurable ones, and learn to improve an answer in two or three moves instead of ten.

## Learn

**Specificity**

- Swap fuzzy words for numbers and examples. "Short" becomes "under 40 words". "Professional" becomes "the tone of a bank's official announcement".
- Name the audience. "For a 60-year-old market trader" and "for a senior engineer" produce completely different answers.
- Say what you *don't* want. "No hashtags, no emojis, no exclamation marks" is often faster than describing what you do want.

**Iteration**

- The first answer is a draft. People who get great results aren't writing magic first prompts. They're better at the second and third message.
- Give editor feedback: **keep** this, **cut** that, **change** this other thing, and say **why**.
- Ask the model to critique itself before you do.
- **Know when to restart.** If a chat has gone off the rails, start a new one and paste in a better prompt that includes what you learned. Arguing with a confused conversation rarely works.

## Exercises

**1. Vague vs specific.** Send these as two separate chats:

```text
Explain what an API is.
```

```text
Explain what an API is to a 60-year-old market trader who uses M-Pesa daily but has never written code. Use one everyday analogy, under 100 words, no jargon.
```

Which one would you forward to a real person?

**2. De-fuzz these words.** Rewrite each vague instruction as something measurable:
- "Make it shorter"
- "Make it more professional"
- "Make it catchy"
- "Add more detail"

Test two of your rewrites on a real piece of text.

**3. The editor's note.** Ask for a 150-word bio for yourself. Then reply with *one* message that includes keep, cut, change, and why. For example:

```text
Keep the first sentence. Cut the part about my hobbies, it's for a conference speaker page. Change the tone to be warmer, like how a friend would introduce me. Keep it under 100 words.
```

Count how many rounds it took. Try to get there in two.

**4. Let it critique itself.** After any answer, send:

```text
Before I give you feedback: list the 3 weakest parts of the answer you just gave me and explain how you'd fix each one. Then rewrite it with those fixes.
```

Was its self-critique right? Did it spot things you would have?

## Level 1 lab: the glow-up log

Collect 10 prompts you've really sent to an AI in the last month. For each one:

1. Rewrite it using Days 1 to 3 (context, specifics, format).
2. Run the old and new versions in fresh chats.
3. Log it in a simple table:

| # | Old prompt | New prompt | What changed | Better? (1–5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | |

Most people find at least three where the difference is embarrassing. Keep this log. It's the first piece of your course portfolio.
